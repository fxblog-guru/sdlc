# User research (G0)

Read this when the problem names users whose needs nobody on the team has asked about, or when the
evidence for an `initiative` is a belief. The G0 checklist row 19 cites this file. Research here is
small and fast: enough to stop building the wrong thing, not a study.

## Principles

| # | Principle | Why | When it does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask about past behaviour, never about the future | "Would you use X?" gets a polite yes. "Tell me about the last time you did Y" gets the truth | — |
| 2 | Five people from the right segment beat fifty from anywhere | Most usability and need problems show up in the first five sessions | Measuring how many, not why: then you need numbers |
| 3 | Name who you talked to | A finding with no source cannot be weighed or rechecked | Anonymous surveys: name the channel and the count |
| 4 | Separate what they said from what you concluded | The quote is evidence; your reading of it is a hypothesis | — |
| 5 | Look for the workaround | A spreadsheet, an email chain or a manual step is proof the need is real and costly | — |
| 6 | Competitors show what exists, not what users need | Study them for gaps and conventions; never copy a feature as evidence | — |
| 7 | Research has a question and a decision it informs | Research with no decision attached is curiosity | — |
| 8 | Disconfirming evidence is recorded | A finding that the problem is small is as valuable as one that it is big | — |

## Trade-offs

| Choice | Choose the first when | Choose the second when |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews · survey | Why, and what you do not yet know to ask | How many, once you know what to ask |
| Usage data · talking to people | The product exists and logs the behaviour | Before it exists, or to explain the numbers |
| Competitor teardown · user interview | Learning conventions and the market's gaps | Learning this user's problem |

## Anti-patterns

| What a junior does | How the reviewer spots it |
|---|---|
| "Users would love this" | No named people, no count, no quote |
| Leading questions | Research notes record "would you use…" questions |
| Opinion as finding | Conclusions with no quote or observation under them |
| Research that only confirms | Every finding supports the plan; nothing against it is recorded |

G0 fails an `initiative` whose research commits one of these.
